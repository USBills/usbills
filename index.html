June 2nd, 2026 to June 5th, 2026

H.R. 8646 (Agriculture, Rural Development, Food and Drug Administration, and Related Agency Appropriations Act, 2027) is a bill sets the annual federal budget for the FDA, USDA, and any nutritional/agricultural programs that the U.S. government implements and funds. The bill essentially dictates how the nation's food policy is shaped for the upcoming year and adds to the federal deficit, setting up contentious arguments on government spending. This bill benefits Republicans as it prioritizes the needs of the GOP, such as reducing agricultural government staffing, cutting climate-change initiatives, and limiting government spending.

H.R. 7892 (No Aid for Ghost Students Act of 2026) is a bill that requires the Department of Education to implement fraud detection for all FAFSA applicants. The bill also mandates the Secretary of Education to prioritize institutions that hand out funding to fraudulent accounts. The bill is important as it combats fraud and completely stops the financial disbursment to bad actors seeking to siphon from the government. This bill benefits legitimate college applicants, American taxpayers, and the universities themselves from having to pay more and protects them from financial fraud.

H.R. 7726 (Stop Child Care Scams Act of 2026) is a bill that mandates the Secretary of Health and Human Services to definitively withhold funding from states that violate federal child-care requirements. The bill is important because it mandates the Secretary to enforce consequences and ensure accountability in state administrators. However, Democrats have argued that the stringent language of the bill could strip vital funding for low-income families and individuals due to administrative errors or mismanagement. The bill mainly benefits taxpayers and the federal government by strengthening accountability and ensuring that federal funding is not wasted.

H.R. 8872 (Preventing Waste, Fraud, and Abuse in TANF Act) is a bill that amends the Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) Act to implement stricter guidelines and combat fraud when the federal government approves block grants. The bill requires the Department of Health and Human Services to track down improper payments and present a 10-year plan to prevent them. It also implements an income cap and gives states specific timelines to spend their TANF funding in order to prevent hoarding. The bill benefits taxpayers by ensuring fraud is combatted and low-income families by ensuring their TANF funding is only allocated to them in a timely manner.

H. Con. Res. 86 is a resolution that forces the President to remove all United States military forces from hostilities with Iran and limits the current administration's power. Unless Congress explicitly declares war or passes the Authorization for Use of Military Force joint resolution, the President is not allowed to engage in foreign wars. The resolution benefits Congress by protecting its oversight on the Executive Branch and American taxpayers who are not in favor of and do not support the war with Iran.

H. Con. Res. 84 is a resolution that forces the President to remove all United States military forces from hostilities with Lebanon and limits the current administration's power. This resolution specifically points to section 5(c) of the War Powers Resolution which allows Congress to remove U.S. troops without the President's approval. The resolution benefits Congress by protecting its oversight on the Executive Branch and American taxpayers who are not in favor of and do not support the war with Lebanon.

H. Res. 518 is a resolution that provides consideration for the $8 billion H.R. 2913, the Ukraine Support Act, which bypasses committee bottlenecks and brings the bill for a speedy vote. The resolution mainly benefits Ukraine by speeding up an arms package for the United States to send to Ukraine in its fight against Russia.
