September 14th, 2026 to September 17th, 2026

H.J. Res. 210 is a joint resolution that blocks an EPA rule that allowed the state of California to enforce its state pollution control standard on ocean-going vessels that are docked at the state's ports. The resolution is important because it is a classic case of federal regulation vs. state authority which challenges California's position under the Clean Air Act. The joint resolution mainly benefits the maritime and shipping industry in California given that they are now shielded from financial penalties and the transition from fossil fuels to cleaner energy sources.

H.J. Res. 213 is a joint resolution that nullifies the EPA's decision to allow the state of California to enforce its strict emission regulations on commercial harbor craft. The joint resolution mainly benefits the maritime and shipping industry in California since harbor craft would now be able to dock and operate in California without incurring financial penalties for their use of fossil fuels.

H.R. 9576 (National Fraud Enforcement Division Act of 2026) is a bill that aims to establish a National Enforcement Fraud Division within the Department of Justice (DOJ) which would be headed by the Assistant Attorney General. The bill is important because it would allow a dedicated division to tackle fraud that targets the federal government, national programs, and the American public while simultaneously coordinating multi-agency fraud investigations. The bill primarily benefits the American taxpayer and the federal government by protecting public funds from waste and criminal abuse.

H.R. 10326 (Preventing Rip-offs and Obtaining Oversight of Funds Act) is a bill that mandates state agencies to share specific records with the U.S. Attorney General upon request in order to protect state-administered federal programs from fraud. The Office of the Attorney General would comply with the Privacy Act and HIPAA to protect citizen data while also submitting an annual report to both the House and Senate Judiciary Committees on what records were requested. The bill is important because it would allow for greater coordination between the state and federal government in investigating fraud in federal programs. The bill primarily benefits the American taxpayer and the federal government by providing them with the data needed to tackle criminal fraud and abuse.

Senate amendments to H.R. 5334 are amendments to the SEED Act that attach new tariff and sanction authorities aimed at Russia. Any nation that obtains sanctioned Russian gas or oil would be subjected to tariffs on all goods from any sector imported to the United States. It would give the President unprecedented tariff power and massively escalate geopolitical tensions. These amendments primarily benefit the nation of Ukraine along with U.S. foreign policy hawks who advocate for greater action against Russia's war machine.

H. Con. Res. 93 is a concurrent House resolution that mandates the President to cease all hostile activities with Iran and withdraw U.S. troops from fighting. While it does not have the power of a law, it does express the will of Congress to cease the war with Iran.
