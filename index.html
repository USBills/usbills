H.R. 1041 (Veterans 2nd Amendment Protection Act) is a bill that stops the VA from automatically reporting a veteran to the National Instant Criminal Background Check System (NICS) if they use a fiduciary to manage their benefits. In other words, the bill protects veterans and their 2nd Amendment rights by protecting their personal information when they need to manage their money. This bill benefits veterans who use fiduciary services by protecting their rights to due process and veterans who previously avoided seeking medical/financial benefits from the VA out of fear of losing their right to bear arms.

H.R. 6047 (Sharri Briley and Eric Edmundson Veterans Benefits Expansion Act of 2026) is a bill aimed at increasing payouts to severely disabled veterans and surviving family members by adding $10,000 annually to their special monthly compensation. It also adds a 1% raise to the cost-of-living adjustments and increases dependency and indemnity compensation for military families who lost a service member. However, in order to fund the payouts, the bill proposes to end the VA home loan funding fee exemptions for veterans who are 70% disabled or less which has drawn opposition from veteran benefits groups. This bill benefits severely disabled veterans and the surviving families of deceased service members by increasing their government payouts.

H.R. 1329 (Smithsonian American Women’s History Museum Act) is a bill authorizes the construction of the Smithsonian's Women's Museum to be built on the National Mall. The bill specifically mentions that the museum is only dedicated to "biological women", mandate that exhibits showcase the diversity of political viewpoints women held, gives the President authority to override the Smithsonian's recommended location, and dropped the passage of H.R. 1330 (Smithsonian National Museum of the American Latino Act) as a requirement for its passage. The bill mainly benefits the GOP and the incumbent President by excluding transgender women along with diversity when showcasing women's history.

H.R. 4312 (SCORE Act) is a bill that provides college athletes a uniform standard on how they can profit off their Name, Image, and Likeness (NIL). The bill explicitly states how student-athletes are not employees of their universities or athletic conferences, protects their rights to enter NIL agreements and use agents, and requires universities that make more than $20 million from athletics revenue to provide long-term medical care, sports-related injury counseling, and academic assistance. The bill also prevents student-athlete unionization to barter for better wages and gives the NCAA an antitrust exemption to enforce its rules. The bill benefits the NCAA, sports-reliant universities, and athletic conferences by shielding them from large financial fees, legal risks, and antitrust lawsuits.

H. Con. Res. 86 is a resolution that forces the President to remove all United States military forces from hostilities with Iran and limits the current administration's power. Unless Congress explicitly declares war or passes the Authorization for Use of Military Force joint resolution, the President is not allowed to engage in foreign wars. The resolution benefits Congress by protecting its oversight on the Executive Branch and American taxpayers who are not in favor of and do not support the war with Iran.
