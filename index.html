H.R. 8312 (Fraud Prevention and Accountability Act) is a bill that sets up an independent watchdog in the Department of the Treasury for fraud prevention. The office will be officially titled the Office of the Inspector General for Fraud, Accountability, and Recovery (OIGFAR) and it will share fraud data within the federal government and enforce pre-payment verification before payments exceeding $50,000. Through this bill, fraud response is shifted from a temporary emergency response to an institutionalized one. This bill benefits oversight agencies, the Department of the Treasury, and private tech vendors by enforcing fraud oversight through data analytics contracts.

H.R. 8464 (Stopping Fraudulent Payments Act) is a bill that aims to combat fraud by requiring government agencies to halt payments that pose an elevated risk of fraud and requires the Department of the Treasury to return payment vouchers for correction if flagged by their "Do Not Pay" system. The bill also shields government employees from personal liability if they delay payments in good faith. The bill is important because it saves government costs and focuses on fraud prevention, not reaction. However, there have been concerns that this bill amasses such a large amount of personal data which increases government surveillance and data security risks. The bill benefits the American taxpayer by ensuring their funds are going to government programs, not stolen by fraudulent payments.

H. Res. 1335 is a resolution that condemns bad actors seeking to defraud the American people and supports reform to federal payments. The resolution combats fraud and lays the foundation for larger anti-fraud legislative reforms. This resolution benefits American taxpayers from the misuse of their funds.

H. Res. 1345 is a resolution that makes the House consider H.R. 8312 and H.R. 8464 in a speedy manner and aims to garner support for the passing of the two bills. This resolution benefits the American public by introducing and quickly passing anti-fraud reforms in order to better protect their funds.

S. 2 (Secure America Act) is a bill that has passed in the Senate and is now in the House for voting. The bill allocates $23 billion to the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), Customs and Border Protection (CBP), and Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). The bill is important because it funds vital new screening technology to stop illicit trafficking into the United States and mandates increased border enforcement. The bill mainly benefits the U.S. Border Patrol and national immigration enforcement by providing them with funding to combat drug and human trafficking.
